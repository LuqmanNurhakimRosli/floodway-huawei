# Permissions and split design gate the benchmark

A reproducible Malaysia flood benchmark can be built before 3 November 2026 from open data, but only if the team stops treating the local datasets as safe to publish. The openly reusable stack is **ERA5 via the Copernicus Climate Data Store (CC BY 4.0 since 2 July 2025, with one older ECMWF page that conflicts)**, **Dynamic World** and **JRC Global Surface Water**, both open with attribution. The Malaysian sources that matter most for a rainfall-driven flood label are gated: DID's historical flood records are **"available on request"**, MET Malaysia station data has **no verified policy**, and no public Malaysian flood/non-flood inventory exists, so the non-flood class must be built and documented by the team. The evaluation must use spatial and year-blocked holdouts, because random splits over district-year rows that share regional weather and neighbouring geography produce optimistic scores, and none of the Malaysian flood papers reviewed reported a spatial split. Ten days is enough for an open-data baseline with a model card and a clean-machine rerun, but not enough to wait for DID or MET replies, so the plan treats those as upgrades. The 2026-27 rulebook was not found online, so the Huawei Cloud deployment requirement rests on the team's screenshots, not on a verified source.

## Open licences cover the physics, not the flood labels

The licence picture splits cleanly between global products and Malaysian government data. Among the global products, **Open-Meteo's API data is CC BY 4.0**, but its free tier is **non-commercial only** with rate limits, and the licence page credits Copernicus rather than naming ERA5 ([Open-Meteo licence](https://open-meteo.com/en/licence); [Open-Meteo pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing)). The **Copernicus licence on the ECMWF page is "Version 1.2 (November 2019)"**, while an ECMWF forum notice says the Climate Data Store licence was replaced by **CC-BY effective 2 July 2025** ([ECMWF Copernicus licence](https://apps.ecmwf.int/datasets/licences/copernicus/); [ECMWF forum](https://forum.ecmwf.int/t/cc-by-licence-to-replace-licence-to-use-copernicus-products-on-02-july-2025/13464)). The notes treat the forum notice as the more recent statement, and the team should cite the CDS dataset DOIs. **ERA5 at about 0.25° is too coarse for urban drainage without downscaling**, though that resolution figure is a standard fact the notes did not verify. **Dynamic World V1** is **CC-BY 4.0**, runs from 2015 at 10 m, and requires a Copernicus Sentinel attribution line ([GEE Dynamic World](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1)). **JRC Global Surface Water** is provided "free of charge, without restriction of use," but its catalogue coverage ends on 1 January 2022 ([GEE JRC GSW](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JRC_GSW1_4_Metadata)). The **Global Flood Monitoring** product has no stated licence or access method in the notes, and its project term ends in October 2026, so it should not be used as a label source.

Malaysian government sources are weaker. **DID's water.gov.my** shows "Copyright (C) JPS 2016. ## Flood labels and non-flood samples decide what the score means

Where the labels come from determines what any accuracy figure means. The notes found **no published, downloadable Malaysian flood inventory with labelled flood and non-flood points**. The nearest items are the RETRACE validation set, which has **350 flood points** (75 Sentinel-2 random points plus 275 DID site-visit records) and states no non-flood samples, and the Omdena rainfall set, which has no documented labels ([RETRACE](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/11/7/378)). The team's keanteng-derived incident set is **presence-only**, so it has flood points and no absences, which means a non-flood class must be built.

The notes give two methods for building that class, both from outside Malaysia. Zhang et al. argue that **the method for generating non-flood samples changes the result**: their best combination of frequency-ratio spatial range and one-class SVM sampling reached an ENSEMBLE AUC of **0.95**, against an unstated baseline ([Zhang et al., Remote Sensing 17(3), 375](https://doaj.org/article/028b6d674ec042039b8d9c92a6d26cb1)). The Odisha study labels non-flood points as areas with **no flood in the previous 20 years**, using 146 flood points from ISRO Bhuvan, but it is not Malaysian and has no data statement ([EPJ Web of Conferences, 2025](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2025/28/epjconf_aimace2025_05016.pdf)). Taken together, the team should draw non-flood points at a distance from every recorded flood (a buffer), from areas with no event in a stated lookback, and should report the sampling rule and its sensitivity. Any non-flood sample drawn near flood points will make classes easier to separate, which is the inflation Zhang et al. describe. Flood-prone bias also needs checking, since a non-flood set drawn from documented areas inherits the documentation bias.

Crowdsourced and event-register sources need separate handling. BencanaBanjir reports have locations and incident details but no non-flood labels ([Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/773950/bencanabanjir-malaysia-crowdsource-database-flood-report)). The RETRACE accuracy numbers come from 350 points across two states, so they are a weak benchmark, not ground truth. Kelantan Sentinel-1 mapping reported only **57 to 60%** overall accuracy, which makes it a caution rather than a reference ([GMJSS Kelantan](https://ejournal.ukm.my/gmjss/article/view/65557)).

## Random splits flatter flood models, so the design must block

The strongest evidence on evaluation design comes from ecology, not flood studies. Roberts et al. simulate spatially autocorrelated data and find that **random k-fold and resubstitution give artificially low error**, while spatial block CV and buffered leave-one-out give error much closer to performance on independent landscapes. They recommend blocks at least as large as the residual spatial autocorrelation range, and in one example residual autocorrelation was negligible beyond about 220 km ([Roberts et al., Ecography 40:913-929](https://www.biometrie.uni-freiburg.de/mitarbeiter/dormann/roberts-et-al-2017-ecography.pdf)). Meyer and Pebesma add an **area of applicability** based on a dissimilarity index between prediction locations and training data, and recommend reporting it with predictions ([Meyer and Pebesma, arXiv 2005.07939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07939)).

The Malaysian and regional flood papers do not test this. **Keya et al. (2024) in Pahang use a single 70/30 split with no cross-validation**, and report an RF AUC of 0.73 or above, but their accuracy is internally inconsistent: the abstract and results give above 0.71 for the top model, while the methods give **0.89** for RF ([PLOS ONE, Keya et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11542787/)). Youssef et al. rank RF first with ROC-AUC 93%, but the full-text split was not readable in the notes ([Natural Hazards 115(2)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/nathaz/v115y2023i2d10.1007_s11069-022-05584-5.html)). Waleed and Sajjad report LightGBM and XGBoost as best, but the notes could not confirm their validation method or metric ([J. Flood Risk Management 18(1)](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/advancing-flood-susceptibility-prediction-a-comparative-assessmen/)). No flood paper in the notes reports random and spatial CV on the same data, so the size of the optimism in flood ML is not measured. The algorithm ranking is therefore weak evidence, and the team should treat algorithm choice as secondary to the split.

For the team's own data, the design implication is specific. The 825 rows are district-year records, so rows share both geography and year. A random split can therefore leak in two ways: neighbouring districts carry spatial correlation, and every district in a flood year shares a regional rainfall shock. The team should hold out **whole regions** (leave-one-region-out by state or basin) and **whole years**, and report both. Holding out by year is an inference from this structure, not a sourced rule. The notes also could not source temporal-split guidance for flood prediction, or the label leakage that target-derived features cause (for example, distance to recorded flood sites). Both are standard concerns that must be checked directly, and both should be in the benchmark's leakage checklist.

Metrics need the same care. The flood papers report ROC-AUC, accuracy, kappa, RMSE and MAE, and one reports prediction time. Flood inventories are usually imbalanced, so AUC alone hides threshold behaviour. The team should report PR-AUC, F1 or a confusion matrix at a stated threshold, and a calibration measure such as the Brier score, with per-region results and intervals. These recommendations are inference: the notes found no source on PR-AUC or Brier use in flood ML.

## Huawei Cloud facts hold, but the region and quota do not

The verified Huawei Cloud facts constrain the design more than the unverified ones. **ModelArts notebooks auto-stop after 1 hour by default, configurable from 1 to 72 hours, with a 5 GB EVS disk mounted at `/home/ma-user/work`**, and both compute and disk are billed until deletion ([Huawei notebook guide](https://support.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/usermanual-standard-modelarts/devtool-modelarts_0004.html)). The account is capped at **10 notebooks** (not adjustable), **training logs are kept only 30 days** unless persistent log saving is set with a dump path, and **batch services run only on public pools** ([ModelArts Service Overview](https://support.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/productdesc-modelarts/ModelArts%20Service%20Overview-pdf.pdf)). Training code goes in an OBS code directory of at most **1,000 files and 5 GB**, with the data kept out of it. The boot file must be a `.py` file, dependencies go in `requirements.txt` on a preset image, and hyperparameters are capped at **100 in total** ([Creating an Algorithm](https://support.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/usermanual-standard-modelarts/develop-modelarts-0003.html); [Creating a Training Job](https://support.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/usermanual-standard-modelarts/develop-modelarts-0010.html)). The **OBS bucket and ModelArts must sit in the same region**, cross-region OBS access is not supported, and encrypted OBS buckets are not supported ([Service Overview](https://support.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/productdesc-modelarts/ModelArts%20Service%20Overview-pdf.pdf)).

Several facts remain open. The **region is uncertain**: the Huawei FAQ lists AP-Kuala Lumpur, updated 16 April 2026, but the region-query page lists Singapore, Jakarta, Bangkok and Manila without it ([FAQ](https://support.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/intl_faq/en-us_topic_0115884695.html); [region query](https://www.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/securecenter/data_protection/region_query.html)). The product page's console link uses `ap-southeast-1`, but the notes could not confirm that this is Singapore, and ModelArts availability in Kuala Lumpur is unconfirmed. **No free tier, student credit or trial quota** was confirmed, because the free-tier page and the billing PDF both failed to load ([Huawei free tier](https://www.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/free/)). The notes found **no scikit-learn example** in Huawei's docs and no scikit-learn in the frameworks the platform names, so the team must confirm a preset Python image includes the right versions. Distributed training is supported only for PyTorch and MindSpore. Huawei's documentation also says **nothing about random seeds**, so seeds must live in the code.

The competition side is thinnest. Huawei's 10th APAC Finals coverage, from May 2026, says nothing about cloud credits, ModelArts or Ascend access, deployment requirements or open-source rules for teams ([Huawei news](https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2026/5/ict-competition-apac-final); [Huawei Malaysia](https://www.huawei.com/my/news/2026/10th-huawei-ict-competition-apac-winners-crowned-at-asean-headquarters)). The 2026-27 rulebook was not found, and no official statement on Gitee or GitHub acceptance was located. The requirement that the project deploy on Huawei Cloud and be open source comes from the team's screenshots, and the team should confirm it against the rulebook before designing around it. The notes also could not read the ModelArts Best Practices PDF, which may contain reproducibility examples.

## Reproducibility and model card fit into ten days

A checkable model card needs the sections Mitchell et al. recommend: **intended use, factors, metrics, evaluation data, training data, quantitative analyses, and caveats**, with confidence intervals on metrics and results broken down by factor ([Mitchell et al., arXiv 1810.03993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.03993)). The Hugging Face template adds an explicit out-of-scope section and evaluation disaggregated by subgroup ([Hugging Face annotated model card](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/model-card-annotated)). For this benchmark, the factors are region or basin, flood-event type, rainfall tier and held-out year. The minimum checkable card for a three-person team is a short intended-use paragraph, an out-of-scope list, the split definitions with region and year IDs, metric definitions, per-region results with intervals, and a limitations list. The notes found no source that sets a minimum seed count or an interval method, so the team must justify both.

The reproducibility package should let a judge rerun the work from one command. The NeurIPS checklist asks for "the exact command and environment needed to run to reproduce the results" ([NeurIPS checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)). DVC's pipeline records dependency and parameter hashes in `dvc.lock`, and committing it after each run preserves the state ([DVC data pipelines](https://doc.dvc.org/start/data-pipelines/data-pipelines)). MLflow records parameters and metrics, but the notes found no source that it captures the environment or git commit, so those must be written explicitly ([MLflow Tracking](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/ml/tracking/)). The ACM policy's "Results Reproduced" badge, which means a team other than the authors reran the work using the author's artifacts, is the closest standard for a judge's rerun ([ACM Artifact Review and Badging](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/artifact-review-and-badging-current)). Pineau et al. found that dataset confidentiality was a main objection to code release, which is why the open-data-first design matters ([Pineau et al., arXiv 2003.12206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.12206)).

## Conclusion

The benchmark's credibility rests on two decisions the team can make this week without waiting for any Malaysian agency. The first is to scope the public claim to what the licences allow: open global products with attribution, the team's own labels with their provenance documented, and no reuse of DID, MET, Omdena or keanteng material until terms are confirmed. The second is to let the split, not the algorithm, carry the result. Because no Malaysian flood paper in the notes reports spatial or year-blocked validation, a benchmark that does report it would be new, and the same run would show how much random splits overstate the team's own scores. The main unknowns are the Kuala Lumpur region, the Huawei free quota, the 2026-27 rulebook, and the provenance of the 825-row set. Those four should be closed first, since each one changes what the team can honestly claim on 3 November.
