# Cut FloodWay's unsupported claims, win SDP's panel

FloodWay can compete for the Sime Darby Property (SDP) "Township of Tomorrow" prize, but not with the current claims. **Three impact figures (30-50% mortality reduction, 220 million residents, 40% faster evacuations) have no source anywhere in the research, "zero false positives" has none either, and Gemini 1.5 Flash was shut down on 29 September 2025.** Only the loss series and the 54-death toll survive checking. The competitor story fails in two places: Waze and Google Maps do take flood reports and closures into routing, and the official Malaysian apps are no longer "monitoring only" (JPS issues multi-day forecasts, MyDIMS routes to shelters and verifies user reports). FloodWay's defensible gap is narrow: street-level flood timing, evidenced verification of photo reports, and flood-aware routing to Malaysian relief centres (PPS). Each is unproven today. SDP's published rubric puts **25% on measurable impact, 20% on pilotability inside an SDP township, 15% on township-priority fit and 10% on scale across townships**, so about 45% of the score rewards a named township pilot rather than technical sophistication ([SDP official page](https://simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/)). The Random Forest's 74.55% accuracy cannot carry a headline: it is mid-pack against published Malaysian studies, its gap to the Decision Tree is about two test predictions if the test set has roughly 110 rows, and the rainfall data behind it may not match the flood-label period. The T&C make "false or misleading information" a disqualification ground (cl. 25.1), so fixing the numbers is a compliance task as well as a credibility one. Registration closes tomorrow (21 October) and the proposal is due 23 October.

## SDP scores township pilots, not model accuracy

The six published criteria total 100%, and the wording matters more than the labels.

| Weight | Criterion (short label) | What the wording rewards | Implication for FloodWay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | Impact and sustainability value | "Measurable benefits to residents, communities and the environment" | Measured or clearly assumed benefits, with sources; no inflated statistics |
| 20% | Innovation and differentiation | "How it improves upon or differs from existing solutions" | Differentiation must survive a judge opening Waze or MyDIMS (Section 3) |
| 20% | Feasibility and deliverability | "Implementing and piloting the solution within a Sime Darby Property township" | Name a township, a pilot point, a data route and an owner |
| 15% | Strategic fit | "Alignment with the People or Planet theme and SDP's township priorities" | Use SDP's own vocabulary: climate adaptation, resilient communities |
| 10% | Commercial and value multiplication | "Business value and scale across multiple townships or use cases" | A cost-and-benefit story across townships, labelled as assumption |
| 10% | Team, evidence and pitch | "Quality of research, validation, prototype or demonstration, team capability, clarity" | Disclosed methods beat large numbers |

All six wordings come from the organiser's FAQ ([SDP official page](https://simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/)), read through a summarising fetcher, and the T&C let SDP change criteria and scoring "in their sole discretion" with non-appealable decisions ([T&C](https://simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/terms-and-conditions)). The weights that circulated in the team's earlier drafts (Impact 25%, Innovation 20%) match the official page, which retires the earlier "unverified" label. A generic national flood app loses points on the 45% that refers to SDP townships (15% fit, 20% pilot, 10% scale); this is an inference from the wording, not a published scoring note.

SDP's own record makes flood a live topic. It lists flood risk exposure as a key risk and climate adaptation as a material issue, and reports 180 acres of wetlands at Bandar Bukit Raja built "as a flood mitigation strategy" ([SDP Sustainability Report 2023](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/annualreport2023/download/sdp-sr2023.pdf)). Its township page says a 2021 flood prompted the design and that nearby parks "serve as environmental warning indicators during emergencies" ([SDP Bukit Raja spotlight](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/sustainability/urbanbiodiversity/spotlight-bandar-bukit-raja.html)). In November 2024 the CEO called the recurring Bukit Raja flash floods "quite urgent" and "a top priority" ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/734847)). Residents told reporters in May 2024 of flash floods "at least three times a year" since December 2021 ([Sinar Harian](https://www.sinarharian.com.my/article/666626/edisi/selangor-kl/penduduk-bandar-bukit-raja-hadap-banjir-kilat-tiga-kali-setahun)) and complained that councils and developers blame each other ([FMT](https://freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2024/05/24/developers-local-councils-need-to-hash-out-flood-issues-say-bandar-bukit-raja-folk)). The leadership panel knows this story. A pitch that ignores it looks naive and one that blames SDP looks risky. The safe framing is FloodWay as the digital layer that complements physical assets (wetlands, ponds, the parks SDP already calls warning indicators), which is the team's idea derived from these facts, not something SDP has requested.

Three further constraints shape the deck. First, the CEO frames SDP's role as "real estate as a value multiplier" ([The Edge City & Country](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/763883)), so the 10% commercial line deserves SDP's vocabulary even at low weight; the research found no verified RM benefit figure, so every value number must be labelled an assumption. Second, SDP's only documented precedent, the Concept Home 2030 competition, rewarded integrated concepts with a route to a real prototype or commission ([StarProperty](https://www.starproperty.my/news/property-news/sime-darby-property-concept-home-2030-competition-winners-announced-/122040)); that is one data point from an architects' contest. Third, the T&C vest submission IP in SDP on creation (cl. 11.1) and require Background IP to be licensed to SDP "perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free" (cl. 12.2), require disclosure of "material use" of AI (cl. 13), and require lawfully licensed third-party datasets and APIs (cl. 14) ([T&C](https://simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/terms-and-conditions)). The existing FloodWay FYP code is Background IP, and its use of Gemini triggers the AI disclosure.

Dates that matter: registration closes 21 October, proposal 23 October, judging 24-28 October with the Top 10 announced 28 October, mentoring 2-6 November, final submission 11 November, Top 10 pitch 14 November, final presentation and awards 3 December ([SDP official page](https://simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/)). The Star calls 14 November the "grand finale" and says the top five pitch at Innovation Day in December ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/09/20/sime-darby-property-launches-nationwide-university-hackathon)), so "finals 14 Nov" in the team's documents should read "Top 10 pitch". The prize pool is RM14,000, with RM6,000 for the champion, and standout entries are considered for pilots in live SDP townships, which makes the pilot plan the real prize. No judges are named, so write for an executive and sustainability reader, not an ML specialist.

## Waze, Google and four Malaysian apps already cover most of the pitch

### The two positioning claims fail as written

The draft says Google Maps and Waze do not account for flooded roads, dynamic flood spread or real-time hazard reports. **That is wrong.** Waze lets users report "flood" as a weather hazard ([Waze Help](https://support.google.com/waze/answer/13809605)) and report full road closures, which reroute the reporter immediately and appear for others once more drivers confirm ([Waze Help](https://support.google.com/waze/answer/13753511)). Authorities can push closures, including natural disasters, through the Map Editor, Partner Hub or a data feed ([Waze Partners Help](https://support.google.com/waze/partners/answer/9315015?hl=en)). Google Maps began testing a "flooded road" report in February 2025, with country availability and routing effect unstated ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/02/12/google-maps-test-weather-incident-reports/)). Google activated SOS Alerts in Search and Maps for Malaysia's December 2021 floods ([Google Malaysia](https://malaysia.googleblog.com/2021/12/google-menyumbangkan-lebih-dari.html)), and Flood Hub lists Malaysia as a covered location ([Flood Hub Help](https://support.google.com/flood-hub/answer/16508958?hl=en)), with riverine forecasts up to 7 days ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/a-flood-forecasting-ai-model-trained-and-evaluated-globally/)). Google's new flash-flood model gives 24-hour likelihoods on a 20 x 20 km grid; whether it is live for Malaysia is unconfirmed ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/protecting-cities-with-ai-driven-flash-flood-forecasting/)).

The second claim, that official apps are "reactive, monitoring only, no prediction or evacuation routing", is also outdated. JPS runs a national forecasting centre; it issued 32 flood warning bulletins "two days ahead" in 2022 ([Sinar Harian](https://www.sinarharian.com.my/ampArticle/237578)), and its January 2026 Kelantan forecast came roughly one day ahead at district and river-basin level ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/01/04/heads-up-kelantan-did-warns-of-possible-floods-this-week-across-10-districts-from-jan-5-with-rising-rivers/204203)). The MyPublicInfoBanjir app had a point-of-interest flood forecast for four states in early 2021 ([SoyaCincau](https://bm.soyacincau.com/2021/01/06/aplikasi-my-public-info-banjir-kesan-kawasan-banjir-hujan)). **NADMA's MyDIMS, launched 24 April 2026, shows PPS open/closed status and capacity with navigation, pushes alerts within 25 km of a disaster, and carries user reports of floods and road closures "verified before public broadcast"** ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/04/24/nadma-launches-mydims-app-for-realtime-disaster-alerts-and-reporting/217562)). MyDIMS therefore overlaps two of FloodWay's three headline features. Its routing method and verification method are undescribed, and no forecasting component appears in coverage ([RTM Berita](https://berita.rtm.gov.my/nasional/senarai-berita-nasional/senarai-artikel/aplikasi-mydims-pertingkat-amaran-awal-bencana-secara-bersepadu/)); the claim to defend is therefore "not described as doing", never "does not do".

### What FloodWay can still honestly claim

Three gaps survive. The first is **point-level timing**: Google's flash model is a 20 km, 24-hour product, JPS bulletins are district and basin level, and a UK study of surface-water nowcasting found a predictability limit near 3 hours with hit rates falling from about 0.37 at 1 hour to about 0.1 at 3 hours ([Smith et al. 2026](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/237772/9/Meteorological%20Applications%20-%202026%20-%20Smith%20-%20N%E2%80%90FOREWARNS%20%20Regional%20Nowcasting%20of%20Surface%20Water%20Flooding%20With%20a%203%E2%80%90h%20Lead.pdf)). That cuts both ways: a finer scale is a differentiator, but short lead times are physically limited, so FloodWay's value lies in the last mile (verification, routing, shelter navigation) rather than long lead times. The second is **AI verification of photo reports**: Waze relies on crowd taps and has no reporter pre-qualification, and its flood alerts in a Dallas study were imprecise in location and mostly more than 200 m from recorded high-water marks ([NHESS 2023](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/23/1/2023/)). Yet a Norfolk study found 71.7% of Waze flood reports predicted trustworthy ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10291591)), so the baseline is not poor, and FloodWay owes a measured false-accept and false-reject rate. The third is **flood-aware routing to Malaysian PPS**. MyDIMS navigates to PPS, but nothing found shows it avoiding flooded roads on the way. This gap is credible as design intent and has no field evidence. A single simulation ("100% hazard avoidance" over 100 scenarios) does not count; remove it.

The case for a reliability gap is real but thin. MyPublicInfoBanjir scores 2.8/5 on 29 iOS ratings with reviews citing offline water-level data after heavy rain ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/mypublicinfobanjir/id1532699572)), myCuaca scores 2.2/5 on 226 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/mycuaca/id955289490)), and the official model reportedly showed no flood indication for Selangor during the 10-15 September 2025 rains ([Utusan](https://www.utusan.com.my/nasional/2025/09/model-ramalan-tidak-tunjuk-indikasi-banjir/)). These are small iOS-only samples and one reported miss, so use them as colour. The documented Waze failure is one case: three men were routed onto an already flooded road in Pasir Mas, Kelantan, in November 2024 ([NST](https://master.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2024/11/1139451/three-rescued-after-waze-leads-them-floodwaters)). A single news report is an anecdote, not a rate. Avoid "first" and "only": student projects such as SwiftBeacon (AR evacuation guidance, 2021 hackathon winner) and Teru Teru (reporting plus "prediction", MMU) already claim adjacent ideas ([EduAdvisor](https://eduadvisor.my/articles/apu-students-wins-student-hackathon-2021-with-flood-mitigation-platform), [MMU repository](https://shdl.mmu.edu.my/13171/)).

Recommended replacement sentence: "Waze and Google Maps rely on crowd-confirmed closures and hazard reports and generic regional alerts; neither was found to provide street-level flood timing, AI-verified reports or Malaysian PPS shelter routing. Official Malaysian apps now forecast at district and basin level, list PPS and take verified reports; FloodWay adds [measured contribution]."

## A 74.55% accuracy score is noise until the split is fixed

### What the number can and cannot say

The team's figures (Random Forest 74.55%, Decision Tree 72.72%, Ensemble 65.45%, KNN 66.06%, Logistic Regression 53.93%) were not backed by a notebook the research could read, so everything here is conditional. Three observations come from arithmetic, not from FloodWay's code. **First, 74.55%, 72.73% and 65.45% equal 82, 80 and 72 correct out of 110, while KNN's 66.06% fits 72 of 109 and LR's 53.93% fits 48 of 89**; no single test-set size up to 2,000 reproduces all five. Either the models were scored on different splits or fold-averaged, or the figures were assembled from different runs. If the test set is about 110 rows, the Wilson 95% interval around 74.55% runs from 65.7% to 81.8%, and RF versus DT is a two-prediction gap. Detecting a 1.8-point gap reliably needs thousands of test rows ([Dietterich 1998](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~tgd/publications/dietterich-approximate-statistical-tests-nc1998.pdf) supplies the testing logic). **Second, accuracy is the wrong lead metric for a rare event**: WMO's forecast-verification group warns that fraction correct is "heavily influenced by the most common category, usually 'no event'" and that hit rate can be inflated by issuing more "yes" forecasts ([WMO JWGFVR](https://jwgfvr.github.io/forecastverification/)). **Third, a rare-event model can reach high accuracy by predicting "no flood" almost always**, so the always-"no flood" baseline must appear beside 74.55%.

### The data may not support a rainfall-triggered forecast

The only matching file the research could find is a state and district rainfall CSV for 2000-2010 hosted on Omdena, with columns, row count and licence unstated; the Kaggle listing and its author could not be located ([Omdena Datasets](https://datasets.omdena.com/dataset/flood-dataset-(malaysia))). If the notebook derives 24/48/72-hour rainfall features from monthly district totals, those columns are not observations, and if the flood labels come from 2015-2021, rainfall and labels share no dates. The model would then learn which districts and months flood in general, which is a seasonal susceptibility map, not a rainfall-triggered forecast. The notes could not confirm either premise; the team can settle it in minutes by reading its own notebook, and it is the single most damaging question a judge can ask.

A repair is feasible in hours. Pull hourly rainfall for the 72 hours before each flood incident from ERA5 or ERA5-Land through Open-Meteo (reanalysis grids of about 25 km and 11 km) ([Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)), cross-check with IMERG Final (0.1 degree, CC BY 4.0, free Earthdata account) ([NCAR RDA](https://data.ucar.edu/dataset/85ffb2de-437a-4b7c-9304-88fa35dad8cb)), and sample non-flood dates from the same districts as negatives. Open-Meteo's free tier is non-commercial only, which a competition entry probably satisfies but a subscription or ad-funded product would not ([Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms)). The data.gov.my developer API is not a rainfall source: it serves MetMalaysia forecasts and warnings plus a flood-warning endpoint, with no licence named in the captured pages ([data.gov.my](https://developer.data.gov.my/realtime-api/weather)).

| Study | Data and task | Reported result | Caveat |

| [Ong and Khamis 2026](https://periodical.uthm.edu.my/index.php/ekst/article/view/22111) | 42 Malaysian stations, 4,217 samples, daily flood yes/no | RF accuracy 0.934, LR 0.910, KNN 0.879 | SMOTE on training data, flood label undefined, F1 inconsistent with the table's precision and recall |
| [Hadi et al. 2024](https://irepository.uniten.edu.my/entities/publication/bf27f800-f442-4d7e-b61a-b1119a2793ee/full) | Dungun basin, 1986-2000 | RF 75.61%, KNN 73.17%, LR 48.78% | Figures are exact fractions of 41, so test set is about 41 rows (my arithmetic) |
| [Lim et al. 2025](https://matematika.utm.my/index.php/matematika/article/view/1686) | Puchong flash flood, 15-minute data | RF accuracy 0.50, recall 1.00 | Honest reporting; many false alarms |
| [Handayani et al. 2025](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/jrc/article/download/27546/12217) | Palembang (Indonesia), Sentinel-1 plus rainfall | XGBoost accuracy 99.95%; LSTM walk-forward ROC-AUC 0.646 | Label built from the input features, so the headline is leaky |

The sample is eight studies, not a systematic review. The pattern is clear enough: headline accuracies from 0.50 to 99.95% reflect label definitions, SMOTE and split choices rather than model skill. FloodWay's 74.55% sits near Hadi's RF (75.61%) and far below Ong and Khamis (93.36%), but none of these are comparable. Do not write "competitive with published studies". Ong and Khamis rank LR above KNN, whereas FloodWay's figures put KNN (66.06%) well above LR (53.93%), another reason not to treat the two as comparable. A realistic claim is "above climatology and above a simple rain-threshold rule at the same precision", and the team must earn it.

### Evaluation plan and honest slide wording

Run these steps, which take about 2-4 hours once the data is fixed. Report prevalence and the majority-class accuracy. Use time-blocked and district-held-out splits instead of random ones, because neighbouring rows are not independent ([scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html)); note that the December 2021 floods will fall in any 2020-2021 test block and dominate it. Add three baselines (prior-only, district-by-month climatology, a rain-threshold rule). Report precision, recall, F1, PR-AUC against prevalence, ROC-AUC, Brier skill score against climatology, recall at precision 0.5, and a cluster bootstrap interval resampled by district-year. Compare models by McNemar on pooled out-of-fold predictions. The research ran a tested code outline on synthetic data; it is not a FloodWay result and every cell of the results table is TBD until the team runs it.

Honest slide text, to use once the TBD values are filled: "Random Forest correctly classified 74.55% of held-out cases in an initial random split (test-set flood prevalence: TBD%; always-no-flood baseline: TBD%). On a train-earlier, test-later split: PR-AUC TBD versus TBD for climatology. RF and Decision Tree are statistically indistinguishable; we chose RF because [reason]. Prototype model; not validated for operational warnings; official warnings remain with JPS and MetMalaysia." If the period-matched retraining is not completed, say so: "initial prototype trained on 2000-2010 monthly district rainfall; period-matched retraining in progress."

### The 100-person survey

A convenience sample of 100 supports statements about those 100 people. The familiar plus or minus 9.8 points is the textbook margin for a simple random sample at p = 0.5, and AAPOR states it is "impossible to develop statistically valid margins of sampling error from nonprobability surveys" ([AAPOR](https://aapor.org/statements/understanding-a-credibility-interval-and-how-it-differs-from-the-margin-of-sampling-error-in-a-public-opinion-poll/)). Write "100 respondents recruited through [channels] between [dates]", never "100 surveyed ASEAN residents" as if representative, and report counts with denominators ("62 of 100"), country counts and age bands. If most respondents are UiTM students, the survey says something about UiTM students. Show the full Likert distribution and the exact wording in an appendix. If an interval is wanted, give a Wilson interval labelled "assumes random sampling, which this sample is not". Survey collection should record no names or IC numbers, include a consent screen and an 18+ age gate. The instrument's content and recruitment are unknown to the research, so the survey's role on the slide is limited to illustrating demand, never to proving impact.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we show FloodWay is better" to "which small part of the flood chain does FloodWay do that a judge cannot find elsewhere, and can we measure it by 14 November". Everything else in the draft, including the impact statistics and the competitor dismissals, either fails a one-minute check or invites one. A clean claim set with a named pilot township and measured gaps will score higher on the 45% that SDP ties to its townships, and on the 10% that rewards validated evidence, than a larger set of numbers.

Two findings are easy to miss. The official landscape has moved: MyDIMS appeared in April 2026 with verified user reports and PPS navigation, so any pitch written before then is stale. And the model's weakest point is not its accuracy score but its provenance: until the team can say what the rainfall columns are and which dates they cover, no metric on slide 7 is safe. What remains uncertain is whether FloodWay's three differentiators hold up under measurement; the research found no evidence for or against, and that is the work for the 2-6 November mentoring sprint.
